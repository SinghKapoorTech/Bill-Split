# Sell the trip, not the subscription

Divit should launch as **host-pays freemium with three ways to pay**: a free tier that never limits core splitting, a **per-event Trip Pass (about $4.99, one-time, bought by the organizer, unlocking AI scans for every member of that event)**, and Pro at the current **$4.99/month or $34.99/year**. Pro should come with a **14-day free trial attached to the annual plan only**, and a **lifetime option (about $49.99)** should sit on the paywall as the third choice. Consumable scan credits should not be the main model, and a time-limited trial should not be the front door. Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite costs about **$0.0003 per scan**, so AI cost does not justify metering ([Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). Apple also bars purchased credits from expiring, so a credit balance becomes a permanent liability ([App Store Review Guidelines 3.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). The existing build mostly fits this plan. Five changes are needed: raise the free allowance from 2 to about **5 scans a month**; count only events the user _owns_ against the 2-group cap; guarantee that invitees never see a paywall; add the Trip Pass as a consumable that the backend writes onto the event document; and move the paywall out of onboarding to the moment just after the first successful split. Four forces drive this. Splitwise's 2023 cap on expense entry is the category's defining backlash. Most use is episodic and trip-shaped. Free alternatives (Tricount, Venmo Groups, Apple Cash's AI split) set a $0 anchor for basic splitting. And roughly half of all paid conversions happen on the day of install. The evidence for a trip pass is real but thin: these passes now exist in the market, but no one has published data on how they convert compared with subscriptions. Treat the Trip Pass price, the free-scan cap and the trial as Remote Config experiments, not settled facts.

## Users pay to remove friction, never to unlock the core action

Splitwise ran the category's natural experiment. In about December 2023 it began capping free users at 3–4 expenses a day, later adding a **10-second cooldown** between entries and ads. Its help center now states the limit as **"up to 4 expenses each day"** ([Splitwise KB](https://kb.splitwise.com/pro/what-is-splitwise-pro); [ITVoice](https://www.itvoice.in/splitwise-has-introduced-restrictions-on-the-number-of-free-expenses-users-can-add)). The reaction set a fairness line that every later comment repeats. One user on the SplitPro GitHub project wrote: "Making pro features or asking money to remove ads is fine, But asking money for adding Expenses (Core feature) is frustrating" ([split-pro GitHub](https://github.com/oss-apps/split-pro)). The cap spawned a whole genre of "free Splitwise alternative" launches that was still running on Hacker News in September 2026 ([HN Algolia](https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search?query=splitwise%20alternative&tags=story&hitsPerPage=30)). Open-source Spliit grew from about 200 to **5–6k visits a week** starting the month after the cap ([Spliit stats](https://spliit.app/blog/spliit-by-the-stats-usage-costs-donations)). Splitwise holds **4.0★ from about 29K ratings** on iOS, against **4.9★ for Splid** and **4.8★ for Settle Up**, neither of which caps expense entry ([App Store: Splitwise](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/splitwise/id458023433); [Splid](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/splid-split-group-bills/id991473495); [Settle Up](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/settle-up-group-expenses/id737534985)). The rating gap fits the backlash but does not prove it caused it. The friction did convert some users: one writer said the cooldown annoyed him into upgrading ([Substack](https://jaisonthomas.substack.com/p/this-app-converts-free-users-to-paying)). But that is a single anecdote set against years of goodwill lost.

The free baseline has also fallen to zero. Tricount, now owned by bunq, **discontinued its Premium tier** and pays for itself by steering users to bunq's card and bank account ([Tricount help](https://help.tricount.com/d/61452)). Venmo Groups (2023) tracks shared expenses for free ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/11/14/venmo-gets-a-new-way-to-split-expenses-among-groups-like-clubs-teams-trip-buddies-and-more)). Apple Cash's Split the Bill now scans and itemizes receipts with Apple Intelligence, with no fee mentioned, but only on iOS and only in the US ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/127565)). Willingness to pay is low even for the category leader, whose revenue estimates run only from **$1M to $10M a year** ([Levels.fyi](https://levels.fyi/companies/splitwise); [Lazyweb](https://www.lazyweb.com/company/splitwise)). Two things follow for Divit. First, manual entry, people, balances, settle-up, Venmo hand-off and share links must stay unlimited and free. Second, the paid layer has to sell what free platforms don't cover: **AI itemization that works on Android and the web, multi-receipt trip ledgers, and no-download links for friends**. Users also tell Splitwise that Pro's charts and extras are "not worth it" ([JustUseApp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/458023433/splitwise/reviews)), so analytics will not carry a paywall. AI scanning will, provided it is accurate. A paid scanner that misses items earns exactly those reviews.

## Every candidate model exists in the market, and the episodic ones fit trips best

The notes disagreed on one point. The sentiment research found "no evidence of any mainstream split app selling a per-trip pass." The competitor audit found three. That gap reflects the sentiment search coming up empty, not an absence in the market, and the App Store listings settle it. **Settle Up** sells Group Premium passes at **$5.99 for 1 week, $9.99 for 2 weeks, $14.99 for 1 month and $39.99 for 1 year, plus $149.99 lifetime**, alongside per-user Premium at $3.99/month or $19.99/year ([App Store: Settle Up](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/settle-up-group-expenses/id737534985)). **Splitwise** bundles a **Trip Pass** with its annual plans: Pro for every member of one chosen trip group for 30 days, once a year ([Splitwise KB](https://kb.splitwise.com/pro/what-is-splitwise-pro)). **Banana Split** sells "Unlock this event" for **$1.99** ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id497258676)). The sentiment researcher's narrower claim does still stand. No user discussion asking for per-trip pricing was found, no conversion data exists for passes against subscriptions, and Settle Up has not officially confirmed whether its Group Premium covers every member. Passes are a proven product shape. Their demand is an open question.

The case for a pass rests on how people use these apps. One traveller wrote in August 2026 that "a monthly or annual subscription doesn't align with the needs of most travellers who take infrequent trips," and paid Splid's one-time fee instead ([packinglighttravel](https://packinglighttravel.com/?p=9731)). Settle Up's own listing pitches Premium at "power users and frequent travelers," which concedes that occasional users won't subscribe ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id737534985)). Subscription fatigue in small utilities is a recurring theme in the tech press, with users saying they would rather pay "$3.99 once" ([Android Headlines](https://www.androidheadlines.com/2025/05/breaking-up-with-your-apps-what-soaring-subscription-cancellation-trends-reveal-about-consumer-habits)). Trips are also bursts of many receipts in a few days, which is exactly when a monthly scan cap bites. That makes the cap a natural point to offer a pass rather than a source of resentment.

The table below scores each structure against Divit's facts: a solo developer, episodic and social use, near-zero AI cost, and RevenueCat already wired for entitlements.

| Model                                     | Market precedent                                                                    | Fit for Divit                                                                       | Verdict                                                                      |
| ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Time-limited free trial as the front door | None of the audited splitters use one; "N free uses" is the norm (competitor audit) | Episodic use means a 3–7 day trial often expires before a second bill               | **No** as the entry point; **yes** as a 14-day trial on the annual plan only |
| Freemium, capped AI, then Pro             | Splitty (3 free), Splitfair (5/month), Splitwise (scan is Pro-only)                 | Matches marginal-value gating and keeps the viral loop open                         | **Yes, the backbone**                                                        |
| Consumable scan credits                   | BillSplit (3 free, packs $1.99–$24.99)                                              | Wallet friction at the table; credits can't expire on iOS; cost does not justify it | **No**                                                                       |
| Per-event Trip Pass                       | Settle Up Group Premium, Splitwise Trip Pass, Banana Split $1.99                    | Maps onto Divit's `events`; organizer pays, everyone benefits                       | **Yes, add it**                                                              |
| Lifetime                                  | Splitty $24.99, Trispend $19.99, Settle Up $149.99 per group                        | Safe at about $0.0003 per scan; answers subscription fatigue                        | **Yes, as the third option on the paywall, with a fair-use clause**          |

Sources for the table: [Splitty](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/splitty-bills-made-easy/id1499263885), [Splitfair](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6744828986), [BillSplit](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/billsplit-ai-receipt-scanner/id6746359329), [Trispend](https://appshunter.io/ios/app/trispend-split-trip-expenses/id6752214370).

Credits lose for reasons beyond friction. Receipt and expense apps overwhelmingly pair a monthly free allowance with a subscription; Expensify, for example, gives about **25 free SmartScans a month** ([CostBench](https://costbench.com/software/expense-management/expensify/free-plan)). Lensa, the best-known credit-pack app, drew complaints that it bills the subscription and the packs separately ([Plisio](https://plisio.net/ai/lensa-ai)). The cost argument also fails on the numbers. A typical scan on Divit's current model uses about 1,282 input and 500 output tokens, about **$0.33 per 1,000 scans**. The 15% store commission on a single $2.99 sale pays for roughly **1,350 scans** ([Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). Telling users "credits because AI is expensive" would be untrue, and they would notice.

## Concrete changes to the plan already built

The current build has a free tier of 2 scans a month and 2 active groups, and Pro at $4.99/$34.99 through RevenueCat with `paywall_enabled` off. That is closer to right than wrong. Both caps are already Remote Config keys (`free_scans_per_month` and `free_active_groups` in `shared/monetizationLimits.ts`), so most of the changes below are config, not code.

| Element                                    | Current | Recommendation                                                                                                 | Why                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Free AI scans                              | 2/month | **5/month** (A/B test 3 vs 5)                                                                                  | 2 covers one dinner and leaves nothing for a second, so first-month value shows up only weakly; peers give 3 lifetime to 5/month to about 25/month; cost per scan is negligible                                                        |
| Free active groups                         | 2       | **Keep 2, but count only events the user owns and that are unsettled**; joined events never count              | Splid gates at the group level and holds a 4.9★ rating, but one reviewer was annoyed at being prompted to pay when _invited_ to a second group ([JustUseApp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/991473495/splid-split-group-bills/reviews)) |
| Manual entry, people, settle, share, Venmo | Free    | **Free and unlimited, permanently and publicly**                                                               | The Splitwise lesson; also Apple 3.1.2's rule against taking away functionality users already have                                                                                                                                     |
| Invitees (share link, joined members)      | —       | **Never shown a paywall**; their actions never consume the owner's quota                                       | Host-pays model (Calendly, Partiful); invitees are the acquisition channel ([Sacra, Partiful](https://sacra.com/research/partiful))                                                                                                    |
| Trip Pass                                  | None    | **Add: $4.99 one-time per event**, unlimited scans for all members inside that event, permanent for that event | Priced at one month of Pro, so repeat users see a better deal in the subscription; well below Settle Up's $14.99/month group pass; test $6.99                                                                                          |
| Pro monthly                                | $4.99   | **Keep**                                                                                                       | Matches Splitwise and is above Settle Up's $3.99; the cross-category $7.99–$9.99 band does not apply to a low-willingness category                                                                                                     |
| Pro annual                                 | $34.99  | **Keep, with a 14-day free trial attached to this plan only**                                                  | At the 2026 median annual price of $34.80; undercuts Splitwise's $39.99                                                                                                                                                                |
| Lifetime                                   | None    | **Add at about $49.99**, with "fair use, up to N scans/day" in the terms from day one                          | About 1.4× the annual price, against 72% of annual subscribers cancelling in year 1; the category's $15–25 anchors are for apps without AI or event features                                                                           |
| Credits                                    | None    | **Don't build**                                                                                                | See above                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |

The annual trial is the one change with strong benchmark support. RevenueCat's study of trials started August 2025–July 2026 found **annual-plan trial-to-paid of 43% for 10–16 day trials versus 24% for trials of 4 days or less**. First renewal on annual plans was **26.6% with no trial** and **47.5% after a 17–32 day trial** ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/free-trial-length.md)). Fourteen days is the compromise for an AI app. AI monthly plans peak at 10–16 days and drop off beyond that, and a two-week trial still covers a typical trip. The trial goes on the annual plan rather than monthly because annual is where a trial changes renewal the most, and it nudges buyers toward the plan that retains best. Cheap annual plans keep up to **36%** of users after a year ([RC SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/company/the-state-of-subscription-apps-2025-launch.md)).

The Trip Pass needs specific implementation choices. Sell it as a **consumable "Event Unlock."** The RevenueCat webhook (already live) verifies the purchase, and a Cloud Function writes `passUnlockedAt` and `passUnlockedBy` onto `events/{eventId}`. Scans by any member inside that event then bypass their personal quota. Because the unlock is permanent and stored on the server, nothing expires and nothing needs StoreKit restore, which avoids both the iOS no-expiry rule on credits and RevenueCat's warning that consumables attached to entitlements unlock forever ([RevenueCat non-subscription docs](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/platform-resources/non-subscriptions)). Two safeguards are required under this repo's own rules. First, Firestore rules must stop clients from writing the unlock fields, and that must be proven by a rules test that fails before the fix. Second, a refund webhook must revoke the unlock. The other design, a 30-day non-renewing subscription in the style of Splitwise, would make Divit compute expiry and handle restore itself. That is more work for a solo developer and gives no clear benefit. Lifetime should be a **non-consumable** tied to the existing Pro entitlement. On Android this needs RevenueCat SDK 7.11.0 or later, because earlier SDKs consume all in-app purchases (same source). On Google Play, enable grace periods and account hold, because billing failures cause **31% of Play cancellations** against 14% on iOS ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)).

Store fees argue against prioritizing web checkout now. Apple's Small Business Program takes 15% ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20201118005376/en/Apple-Announces-App-Store-Small-Business-Program)). US link-outs currently carry no Apple commission, but a cost-based fee is pending on remand. Google began charging fees on US external payments on October 1, 2026 ([FKKS](https://technologylaw.fkks.com/post/102mp7y/update-your-app-external-purchase-developments-for-apple-google)). The saving is about 12 points before conversion friction, and web revenue is only **3.2%** of the global total ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps/)). Offer Stripe web billing to people already using divit-bill.com, and stop there.

## Onboarding puts the paywall after the first itemized split

Placement matters more than any price. About **50% of paid conversions happen on Day 0** ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)), and **89% of trial starts** do too. Users who miss that first session have about an 11% chance of ever starting a trial ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/how-to-personalize-onboarding-and-paywalls-in-your-mobile-app/)). That favors a first-session paywall. On the other side, hard paywalls convert about **10.7% versus 2.1%** for freemium ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)), but they would put a purchase wall in front of the invited friends who make up much of Divit's install base and who arrive with no intent to buy. Commentators argue that network effects are exactly the case where freemium still pays ([Admiral Media](https://admiral.media/app-paywall-optimization/)). The way to satisfy both is a **soft, dismissible upsell on Day 0, shown right after the "aha" moment**, plus contextual paywalls when the user hits a limit. That combination is what RevenueCat recommends ([RevenueCat paywall guide](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/guide-to-mobile-paywalls-subscription-apps.md)). It also matches the audited competitors: their paywalls sit at a limit or a feature tap, never in onboarding.

Divit has two first-run paths, and they must stay separate.

| Step | Organizer (organic install)                                                                                                                                                                                                            | Invitee (opened a share link)                                                      |
| ---- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | 2–3 value screens at most: "Snap the receipt, tap who had what, Venmo in one tap." Median onboarding is 11 screens ([Lazyweb](https://www.lazyweb.com/research/average-onboarding-screens-2026)), and Divit has nothing to personalize | Lands directly on the bill in the browser or app; no value screens                 |
| 2    | Two big buttons, **Scan a receipt** and **Add items manually**, in guest mode with no account                                                                                                                                          | Claims their items and sees their total; no account needed to view                 |
| 3    | Camera permission requested only on tapping Scan, with one line of context                                                                                                                                                             | —                                                                                  |
| 4    | **Aha moment:** the itemized scan result appears, then people are assigned and totals shown                                                                                                                                            | Pays via Venmo hand-off                                                            |
| 5    | Account requested only to **save, share or send Venmo requests**, as Apple 5.1.1(v) requires ([Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))                                                                  | Optional prompt after paying: "Start your own split, with 5 free AI scans a month" |
| 6    | **Soft upsell after the first completed split:** annual plan preselected with a 14-day trial in timeline style (today full access, reminder on day 12, charged on day 14); monthly and lifetime as alternatives; prominent "Not now"   | **Never shown**                                                                    |
| 7    | Notification permission after the first share, framed as "Know when friends pay you" (and as the trial reminder for anyone who started one)                                                                                            | —                                                                                  |
| 8    | **Contextual paywalls:** at the 6th scan of the month, or when creating a 3rd owned event. _Inside an event_, the Trip Pass leads, with annual as the secondary option                                                                 | —                                                                                  |

The trial presentation must follow Apple's 2026 enforcement. Since mid-January 2026 Apple has been rejecting paywalls with a free-trial on/off toggle under guideline 3.1.2, so attach the trial to a specific plan ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/rip-toggle-paywall.md)). Show the billed amount at least as prominently as the word "free" ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/841626)). Blinkist's timeline paywall with a reminder raised trial starts **23%**, cut complaints **55%**, and lifted notification opt-in from **6% to 74%** ([UX Planet](https://uxplanet.org/how-solving-our-biggest-customer-complaint-at-blinkist-led-to-a-23-increase-in-conversion-b60ad514134b)). Delayed sign-up is supported by evidence too. A Localytics finding of **28% more retained users** when registration can wait comes only secondhand, through a vendor that sells authentication ([Rownd](https://rownd.io/blog/why-you-should-delay-authentication-onboarding-in-your-app)), so read it as directional.

There is a real counter-argument. FitnessAI moved its paywall _before_ onboarding and roughly doubled revenue ([RevenueCat paywall guide](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/guide-to-mobile-paywalls-subscription-apps.md)). That works for intent-driven installs. For Divit it is worth testing only on organic installs, never on link-driven ones. Once `paywall_enabled` is on and there is a baseline, run it as a RevenueCat experiment.

## What to measure, and the numbers that should trigger a change

Benchmarks give rough targets, not promises. Freemium apps median **2.1% download-to-paid by Day 35**. North American apps across all models reach **2.8%**, and median Day-60 revenue per install is **$0.38 for freemium** and **$0.55 for North America** ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026); [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps/)). Only **17.3%** of new apps reach $1K MRR within two years (same source), which is worth keeping in mind for a pre-launch solo product. Track installs separately by acquisition path (organic versus invitee). A blended number will hide whether the invitee loop is working.

Six metrics settle the open questions. **Scan success** (scans finished without the user editing line items) comes first, because an inaccurate scanner sinks every model. **Limit-hit rate** shows whether 5 free scans is too generous: if under 10% of active organizers hit it, tighten toward 3. **Trip Pass share of purchases, and pass-to-subscription upgrades** test the cannibalization risk: if passes take most purchases and repeat buyers keep buying passes, raise the pass price toward $6.99. **Day-0 upsell view-to-trial and trial-to-paid**, compared against RevenueCat's 43% annual benchmark, show whether the post-aha placement works. **Invitee-to-organizer conversion** (invitees who later create their own bill) measures the viral loop that justifies freemium in the first place. **Refunds and involuntary churn** (Adapty puts average annual-plan refunds at 4.2% ([Adapty 2025](https://adapty.io/blog/state-of-in-app-subscriptions-2025-in-10-minutes/))) show whether the trial and timeline disclosure are honest enough. Before scaling, also log Gemini `usageMetadata`, so the $0.0003-per-scan estimate is confirmed against Divit's real receipts.

## Conclusion

Divit's monetization depends more on being trusted than on a clever paywall. A category leader taught a whole market to resent paying for the basic act of logging an expense. Meanwhile Divit's one costly-feeling feature, AI scanning, turns out to cost almost nothing to run. So caps should be set as a deliberate conversion choice and presented as generous, not justified by cost. Divit can make that generosity visible by keeping logging unlimited and keeping invitees free, which costs competitors with per-user caps something real. The Trip Pass is the bet with the most upside and the least evidence. The pass format already exists in the market, but nobody has published how often it sells, so Divit's own data will be the first real test.

The order of work matters for a solo developer. Integrate the purchase SDK with the existing subscription and lifetime products first. Build the post-aha upsell and the owned-events-only group count next. Ship the Trip Pass third, with its rules test and refund revocation. Then switch `paywall_enabled` on for a small cohort of organizers. If Day-0 data shows the post-aha upsell converting near benchmark and the pass not cannibalizing annual plans, the structure holds. If not, every lever except the pass product itself is already a Remote Config value.
